Keep your CPAP running when the power doesn't

A plain-English guide to running a ResMed AirSense 11 on battery while travelling. We use India as the main example: 230 V power, different plugs, and power cuts that can hit in the middle of the night.

The quick answer

  • Use a CPAP battery that works as a UPS. Wall power runs straight through it, and it switches to battery on its own if the power cuts. We use a Zopec Explore Mini (99.5 Wh).
  • Chain it like this: wall → 100–240 V USB-C charger → UPS battery → AirSense 11. See the diagram.
  • Turn the humidifier and heated tube off when you might be running on battery. With them off, we plan on about one night (roughly 10–12 h) from one ~100 Wh battery. That's our planning estimate, not a measured result yet.
  • Keep spare power banks off the night path. Use them during the day to top up the CPAP battery.
  • Flying: batteries of 100 Wh or less generally go in carry-on and never in checked bags. Details.
  • India: 230 V, 50 Hz. The AirSense 11 power supply takes 100–240 V, so you need a plug adapter, not a voltage converter. Details.
  • Test it all at home first. Here's how.

Why this matters

A CPAP running from the wall simply stops when the power goes out, and you wake up to a mask that isn't blowing. In places where power cuts are common, a battery that takes over without a hiccup can be the difference between a full night of therapy and a broken one.

The AirSense 11's own power supply is rated 100–240 V, 50–60 Hz, and ResMed lists typical power use of 56.1 W and a peak of 73.2 W. On battery, the humidifier and heated tube are the biggest thing you control: published runtimes drop from a full night to a few hours with them on (see Batteries compared).
