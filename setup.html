The outage-proof setup

The idea is simple: put a battery between the wall and your CPAP. If the power cuts at 3 a.m., the battery takes over and you keep sleeping.

The night chain

  1. Wall outlet + plug adapter. In India that means a Type D adapter for most sockets (we carry a Ceptics India adapter). See India power basics.
  2. A dual-voltage USB-C charger. We use an Anker 100 W charger. Check the label says 100–240V input. Zopec's manual asks for a 65 W (or equivalent) USB-C charger for this setup, so 100 W leaves some headroom.
  3. A UPS-style CPAP battery. We use the Zopec Explore Mini. Zopec says that while wall power is present it passes straight through (sparing the cells), and if it senses the power has dropped it switches to battery instantly.
  4. AirSense 11, connected with the AirSense 11 DC cable that comes with the Explore Mini.

Zopec's manual also describes a second way to charge it in UPS mode: your AirSense 11's own power cord plus ResMed's AirSense 11 charging adapter. Either way, follow the manual for your exact cables.

Heated tube off. Zopec's manual says that in the USB-C UPS setup the heated tube must be off, or the CPAP will start pulling from the battery even with the wall connected. If you see the battery draining, turn the humidifier and heated tube off too.

Why not just use a power bank?

It's tempting to plug a big laptop power bank into the CPAP and charge the bank from the wall. We don't recommend it for the night:

  • Consumer power banks with "pass-through charging" aren't designed as medical UPS units. When wall power cuts, some briefly drop their output while they switch over, and that blip can be enough to reboot the CPAP mid-night.
  • Every extra adapter and cable in a daisy-chain is one more thing that can wiggle loose or negotiate the wrong voltage.
  • A generic power bank usually needs an extra USB-C-to-CPAP cable, which is another link to trust.

So we keep our EcoFlow RAPID Pro (99.54 Wh) off the night path. During the day, if the CPAP battery ran down overnight, we use the EcoFlow to recharge it over USB-C. That's useful on days when the power is out for a long stretch.

How long will it last?

With the humidifier and heated tube off, we're planning on roughly 10–12 hours, so about one night, from one fully charged ~100 Wh battery. Zopec's own pages say "1–2 nights" or "1.5–2.0 nights" in the same conditions. We use the lower number because runtime depends heavily on your pressure, mask leak and the room.

With the humidifier on, runtime drops a lot. For its similar 95 Wh Pilot-24 Lite, the runtime table a Medistrom retailer publishes says using the humidifier or heated tubing cuts runtime to about 2–4 hours.

We'll replace the planning estimate with our own measured numbers after our tests.

Not medical advice. This is a traveller's guide, not guidance from a clinician. Check your therapy settings and any battery use with your doctor or DME (equipment supplier), and check battery rules with your airline before you fly.
