India power basics

India runs at 230 V, 50 Hz, about twice the US voltage. The good news: modern CPAP and USB-C chargers are built for it. You mostly need the right plug shape and a plan for power cuts.

Voltage: adapter yes, converter no

  • The AirSense 11's 65 W power supply is rated 100–240 V, 50–60 Hz (ResMed user guide). It works on Indian power with just a plug adapter.
  • Your USB-C charger needs to say 100–240 V on the label. For example, Anker lists its 100 W chargers at 100–240 V input. Check yours, because not every charger is dual-voltage.
  • A plug adapter only changes the plug shape. It does not change the voltage, so anything you plug in through it must itself be rated for 230 V.

Plugs: C, D and M

TypeWhat it looks likeWhere you'll see it
DThree round pins in a triangleThe most common domestic plug in India (BIS)
CTwo thin round pins ("Europlug")Low-power, two-pin devices
MThree larger round pinsHigher-current appliances

A US flat-pin plug won't fit. A travel adapter that offers a Type D plug covers most rooms. We carry a Ceptics India adapter.

Power cuts

Power cuts can come without warning and at any hour, sometimes briefly, sometimes for longer. If the home has backup power, check whether it actually covers the outlet by your bed. That's why we put a UPS-style CPAP battery in the chain every night, not just when an outage seems likely.

  • Recharge the CPAP battery whenever power is available during the day.
  • Keep a charged power bank to top it up if the outage runs long.
  • Keep the humidifier and heated tube off on battery to stretch runtime.

We don't give outage frequency numbers here because they vary a lot by city and neighbourhood, and we didn't find one reliable official figure to quote.

Not medical advice. This is a traveller's guide, not guidance from a clinician. Check your therapy settings and any battery use with your doctor or DME (equipment supplier), and check battery rules with your airline before you fly.
