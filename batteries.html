Batteries compared

There are two kinds of battery people use with a CPAP: ones built to sit in the power path like a UPS, and general power banks. Both can be useful, just for different jobs.

What "UPS-style" means

A UPS (uninterruptible power supply) sits between the wall and your device. When wall power is there, it powers the device and stays charged. When wall power disappears, it switches to battery fast enough that the device doesn't notice. Zopec describes three parts: wall power pass-through, outage detection with automatic switching, and the reserve battery.

A note on runtime numbers

Runtime depends mostly on your pressure setting. Here are tested AirSense 11 runtimes for the Pilot-24 Lite with the humidifier and heated tube off, from a table published by retailer Choice One Medical (Medistrom's own page just says "1–2 nights" at an average pressure):

PressureRuntime (published test)
5 cm H₂O20 h 11 m
10 cm H₂O12 h 33 m
15 cm H₂O8 h 55 m
20 cm H₂O6 h 58 m

These are published figures for one battery, not ours, but they show the pattern: higher pressure means a shorter night. Our own planning number for the Zopec is about one night (10–12 h) with the humidifier and heated tube off.

Why all three are just under 100 Wh

That's not a coincidence. 100 Wh is the line under which spare lithium batteries are generally allowed in carry-on without airline approval. See Flying with CPAP batteries.

Not medical advice. This is a traveller's guide, not guidance from a clinician. Check your therapy settings and any battery use with your doctor or DME (equipment supplier), and check battery rules with your airline before you fly.
