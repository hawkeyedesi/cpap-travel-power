Flying with CPAP batteries

The short version: spare lithium batteries go in your carry-on, never in checked bags, and anything at or under 100 Wh is generally fine without asking the airline.

  • Up to 100 Wh: generally allowed in carry-on (FAA, IATA).
  • Over 100 up to 160 Wh: only with airline approval, and no more than two spares per person (FAA).
  • Over 160 Wh: usually not allowed on passenger aircraft (IATA).
  • Never in checked baggage. If your carry-on gets gate-checked, take the batteries out first and keep them with you.

Where our kit lands

Going by the manufacturers' stated capacities, the Zopec Explore Mini (99.5 Wh), Medistrom Pilot-24 Lite (95 Wh) and EcoFlow RAPID Pro (99.54 Wh) are each just under 100 Wh, so they fall in the "generally allowed in carry-on" group.

The FAA sets no quantity limit for most batteries under 100 Wh, but it says many airlines have stricter limits on how many power banks each passenger can carry. Check your airline's page before you pack two or three.

Packing tips

  • Protect the terminals from short circuits: original packaging, a battery case or pouch, or tape over exposed contacts (FAA, IATA).
  • Make sure the Wh rating is printed on each battery. If it isn't, the FAA says Wh = volts × amp-hours. Carrying a printout of the spec page can help at security.
  • Don't fly with a swollen, damaged or recalled battery (FAA, IATA).
  • Empty the humidifier tub before packing the CPAP.

Flights to, from or within India

In November 2025, India's aviation regulator (DGCA) issued an advisory that power banks and spare batteries must stay in hand baggage, and that using or charging power banks on board is prohibited, including from the seat's power outlet. It also says not to put them in overhead bins. Expect cabin crew on these flights to enforce this.

If you plan to use your CPAP on the plane on a long flight, ask the airline ahead of time. ResMed's user guide says the AirSense 11 meets FAA requirements for all phases of air travel, but whether you can run it on board, and from what power source, is the airline's call.

What we couldn't confirm

Rules about CPAP machines as medical devices (for example whether they count toward your cabin bag allowance) vary by airline and country. We haven't listed them here because we couldn't confirm one rule that applies everywhere. Ask your airline.

Not medical advice. This is a traveller's guide, not guidance from a clinician. Check your therapy settings and any battery use with your doctor or DME (equipment supplier), and check battery rules with your airline before you fly.
